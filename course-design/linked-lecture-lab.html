Linked Lecture–Lab Coordination

Starting in Fall 2025, lecture and lab were combined under a single CRN. A course whose lecture and lab had different instructors now had to run out of one Canvas shell and one syllabus. I saw that as a chance to work with the other instructors on a smooth transition. The steps are below.

That fall I taught the lecture for two linked courses. In CHEM V101 (CRN 70961) the lab was taught by Zhibang (Jim) Duan. In CHEM V104 the two lab sections were taught by Antonia Flores (71293) and Crystal Serrano (71290). The linked format has continued since.

Step 1 — The department meeting (April 2025)

On April 18, 2025, the chemistry department held a department meeting on how lecture and lab would be integrated for Fall 2025 and how the shared Canvas shells would work. Two of its decisions apply to the linked courses:

  • Lecture instructors would set up and maintain the shared Canvas shell. Lab instructors would be added as co-instructors, mainly for grading and due dates.
  • The final grade would be split between lecture and lab by unit count rather than a fixed percentage. For CHEM V101, a four-unit lecture with a one-unit lab, that meant 80:20.

Several questions were left open, including minimum passing grades for each component, make-up labs, and how to grade late work.

The reasoning in the room was that other departments already weighted their labs this way. Physics and microbiology, with one-unit labs, give the lab 20–25% of the grade. We agreed to try the unit rule for a year and adjust it if it did not work.

Because I was teaching the lecture for both V101 and V104 that fall, I wanted the details settled early. No weekday meeting time worked for everyone in the V101 group, so I proposed that we agree in writing first and meet only if that failed, and I sent a draft grading structure built on the meeting's decisions.

Step 2 — Opening the conversation with the lab instructors (July 19, 2025)

The April meeting recorded decisions, and I carried them into the July emails, the shared shells, and the syllabi. The meeting could not say who would teach each lab. That was settled only in July, after the department hired a new full-time faculty member:

  • July 8 — Crystal Serrano joins the department as full-time faculty.
  • July 11 — the revised Fall instructor assignments go to the division office.
  • July 19 — I write to the lab instructors for both of my courses: Antonia and Crystal for V104, and Jim for V101.
  • August 11 — the semester begins.

That left a little over three weeks to agree on grading, build shared shells, and finish syllabi, so I wrote as soon as I knew who I would be working with.

Before writing, I pulled together the two points from the April meeting that mattered most for a shared course, grading and the Canvas setup, so the lab instructors had something specific to respond to. For grading I gave the one fixed rule, the lecture-to-lab ratio, and said that as long as it held, each of us could keep teaching our part the way we already did. For Canvas I attached screenshots of my lecture and lab modules so it was clear exactly what I meant by putting both in one shell.

Under the April unit rule, V101's lab would have counted for 20% of the grade. At 20%, what a student does in lab barely moves the final grade, so I used 70:30 for my sections, and the instructors the emails describe as agreeing to 7:3 are the lab instructors for those sections. Jim replied that he had not planned the lab modules yet and would follow the setup.

Step 3 — Adjusting the Canvas layout together (July 26–28)

After the first emails I built a sample shell using V101, grouping modules by category (lecture, lab, general) with an icon for each, and sent it to Antonia and Crystal.

Crystal raised a problem I had not considered. Lecture and lab do not move in step; a lab can come a week or two before or after the matching lecture topic, and a category archive hides that. Antonia said she opens her lab material week by week. So we added a second layout: modules titled "Week 1, Week 2…", with text headings separating lecture and lab inside each week. Either layout was acceptable as long as titles and icons were consistent. Crystal also asked that the two V104 lab sections weight their categories the same way, and we agreed.

Step 4 — Adjusting the syllabus together (July 22 – August 11)

The syllabus went through the same back-and-forth. I drafted a sample syllabus for the combined V104 course and sent it around. Antonia added assignment descriptions and the lab schedule, I updated both section versions, and Crystal checked the schedule for her section. On August 11 I submitted all four syllabi (two V101, two V104) to the division office, copying the lab instructors. Each one gives the final grade as 70% lecture and 30% lab.

Step 5 — The semester

Once the setup was in place, the semester ran without much friction, and each of us taught our part in our own style. I think that was mostly because the places where we might have clashed had been handled in the system beforehand. Antonia, for example, drops the two lowest scores in her lab categories, and I do not drop any in lecture. Because each category in Canvas carries its own weight and its own drop rule inside the 70:30 split, both practices could sit in the same gradebook without anyone having to give theirs up.
